# Why General Fund transfers-in spiked in FY2022 and FY2023

Source documents: FY2022 ACFR and FY2023 ACFR (Note 8, "Interfund Receivables, Payables and
Transfers," plus Management's Discussion and Analysis). Both years show transfers into the
General Fund far above the roughly $15M the County normally budgets for this line — and in
each case the ACFR's own budget-to-actual comparison confirms the size of the anomaly: the
County budgeted $16,934,188 in transfers-in for FY2022 and $14,697,405 for FY2023, both close
to the "norm" cited in the research question, before actual transfers-in came in at $55,682,134
and $45,047,040 respectively.

## FY2022 — cause IS documented

**Total transfers in to General Fund: $55,682,134** (Note 8 table, ACFR p.70)

Counterparty breakdown (Note 8 table):
- COVID Relief Fund → General Fund: **$41,245,916**
- Nonmajor Governmental Funds → General Fund: $12,436,218
- Internal Service Funds → General Fund: $2,000,000

**Documented cause:** the COVID Relief Fund transfer is the entire excess over the norm
($55.7M − $41.2M ≈ $14.5M, which lines up with the $16.9M budgeted/normal level). The ACFR
states this cause in two places.

Note 8 narrative (ACFR p.70):

> "During fiscal year 2022, the County transferred $41 million from the COVID Relief Fund to
> the General Fund. These funds served to replace revenue lost during the COVID-19 pandemic."

MD&A Financial Highlights gives a fuller breakdown of the same $41M (ACFR p.4-5):

> "The COVID relief fund was created to account for the proceeds directed to the County by the
> Department of the U.S. Treasury. Approximately $41,000,000 in total was transferred to the
> General Fund. Approximately $27,000,000 was for lost revenue replacement, $2,600,000 for
> COVID expenditures and supplies, $4,400,000 for overtime paid to EMS, Sheriff and Detention,
> $4,300,000 for Greenville County technology upgrades and $3,267,000 for premium pay for
> public safety and other frontline responders."

So FY2022's spike is a one-time, federally-sourced event: American Rescue Plan (Coronavirus
State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds, per the Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards
elsewhere in the ACFR) money booked in a COVID Relief special revenue fund, then moved into
the General Fund and spent there on revenue replacement, COVID supplies, frontline overtime
and premium pay, and technology upgrades.

The MD&A separately notes the County also recorded $16,544,108 in transfers *out* of the
General Fund in FY2022 (to Debt Service, a matching-grant fund, a special revenue fund, and
$10,476,212 to the Health and Dental internal service fund) — not part of this question but
included for completeness (ACFR p.11).

## FY2023 — cause is NOT documented for most of the increase

**Total transfers in to General Fund: $45,047,040** (Note 8 table, ACFR p.67)

Counterparty breakdown (Note 8 table):
- Revenue Bonds Fund → General Fund: **$30,336,733**
- Nonmajor Governmental Funds → General Fund: $12,710,307
- Internal Service Funds → General Fund: $2,000,000

**Not documented in the ACFR.** Unlike FY2022, the ACFR gives no sentence explaining why
$30,336,733 moved specifically from the Revenue Bonds Fund into the General Fund. The only
narrative language attached to Note 8 that year addresses a *different* pair of transfers out
of the same two source funds — money that went to the Capital Projects Fund, not the General
Fund:

> "During fiscal year 2023, the County transferred funds requisitioned from bond proceeds to
> the Capital Projects Fund to cover expenditures related to the new county office building and
> parking structure. The Greenville County Redevelopment Corporation and the Revenue Bonds Fund
> transferred $20 million and $39 million to the Capital Projects Fund, respectively. The
> majority of the other transfers were established by the budget ordinance." (ACFR p.67)

Those two amounts ($19,523,429 to Capital Projects from GC Redevelopment Corp, and
$39,460,334 to Capital Projects from Revenue Bonds) are confirmed in the Note 8 table, but they
are separate line items from the $30,336,733 that the same table shows moving from Revenue
Bonds to the *General* Fund. The ACFR never states a purpose for that $30.3M piece — it falls
under the generic catch-all in the same paragraph, "the majority of the other transfers were
established by the budget ordinance," which describes a mechanism (the transfer was authorized
in the budget) rather than a cause (why the money moved).

The MD&A adds one adjacent data point but still no stated reason for the transfer itself: the
Revenue Bonds fund balance fell from $66,234,960 (FY2022) to $19,730,896 (FY2023), which the
MD&A attributes only to construction spend-down, not to the transfer to General:

> "The Revenue Bonds fund balance for 2022 was $66,234,960 compared to $19,730,896 for 2023.
> This decrease in fund balance resulted from drawdowns of construction project funds held with
> the trustee related to the new County administration building and parking garage." (ACFR
> p.12)

That sentence explains the fund's overall balance decline (bond proceeds being spent on the
County office building/parking garage project) but does not connect it to the $30.3M that
specifically moved to the General Fund rather than being spent directly by the Revenue Bonds
Fund or routed to Capital Projects.

The MD&A's Financial Highlights bullets for FY2023 (ACFR p.4-5) — which is where FY2022's
COVID narrative lived — instead list three bond transactions with no reference to a General
Fund transfer: a $55,650,000 Installment Purchase Revenue Bond Anticipation Note (refunding the
2022 note and funding County Administration building furnishing/equipping), a $6,000,000
General Obligation Bond for a Magistrate Court facility, and a $27,970,000 Special Source
Revenue Bond to reimburse prior Woodruff Road construction costs. None of these bullets states
that bond-fund transfers drove the General Fund transfers-in total.

**Bottom line for FY2023:** the size and counterparty of the anomaly are documented (Revenue
Bonds Fund is clearly the source of the excess), but the *reason* $30.3M of bond-related
transfers landed in the General Fund specifically — as opposed to Capital Projects, where the
rest of that year's Revenue Bonds and Redevelopment Corp transfers went — is not documented in
the ACFR. Reporting a specific cause (e.g., "reimbursing the General Fund for prior capital
outlay" or similar) would be speculation not supported by the source text.
